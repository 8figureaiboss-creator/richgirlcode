# Niche Markets and Compliance: 8 Figure AI, Business Rehab.OS, Options Traders Academy (US, 2024-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-05. This is not legal advice. It records what the rules and cases say, citing primary sources where they were reachable. ## 1. AI implementation agencies (8 Figure AI): pricing, client acquisition, retainer churn, productization

### Takeaway
2026 benchmarks put SMB AI-agent builds at about $1,500-$12,000 one-off and light-support retainers at about $500-$3,500/mo. 8 Figure AI's $5K build and $600/mo maintenance fall in the mid-market range for the build and the low-to-mid range for the retainer. Most retainer churn happens in the first 90 days and comes from value the client cannot see (no reporting or ROI), not from price. All of these benchmarks come from vendor or agency blogs, not audited surveys.

### Cited Findings
- Small-business AI retainers are estimated at $500-$1,500/month in one 2026 guide. Another gives $1,000-$3,500/mo for 1-2 workflows, including build and light maintenance. A third lists a "Starter" tier at $1,200-$2,500 for one agent plus one workflow. — [Codewave](https://codewave.com/feeds/blog/ai-automation-agency-pricing); [Layer3Labs guide (updated Jul 2, 2026)](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-agency-pricing); [Taskip](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/)
- Setup for a single-purpose AI (voice) agent is put at $1,500-$5,000, and more for complex integrations (Jan 8, 2026). — [Ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/how-much-to-charge-for-ai-voice-agent)
- A basic automation build is estimated at $1,500-$12,000 one-off. Larger providers have project minimums of $25,000-$100,000+, which reflects mid-market and enterprise work. — [DEV Community / ScaleLogix 2026 pricing guide](https://dev.to/scalelogix_ai/how-to-price-ai-agency-services-in-2026-a-complete-guide-for-operators-5f26); [Pickaxe, "Monetize AI agents 2026"](https://pickaxe.co/post/monetize-ai-agents-2026)
- Common pricing models are fixed-fee, retainer, performance-based and hybrid. Some guides favor outcome pricing tied to leads captured, appointments booked or revenue recovered. They also advise SMBs to buy around one workflow outcome rather than a "$25,000 AI infrastructure build." — [Agency Handy](https://www.agencyhandy.com/informative/agency-pricing-in-the-age-of-ai/); [illco blog](https://illco-command.vercel.app/blog/ai-automation-agency-pricing-small-business)
- One site claims a pricing dataset of "9,312 agencies priced" (methodology unverified). — [AI agency pricing calculator](https://ai-agency-pricing-calculator.pages.dev/)
- Churn (AI automation specifically, Jun 2026) has four process causes: the client never saw early value, can't see the ROI, something broke quietly and eroded trust, or never got clear reporting. "Around 70 percent of churn happens in the first 90 days." — [Ciela, Why AI automation clients churn](https://ciela.ai/blogs/why-ai-automation-clients-churn-and-how-to-keep-them)
- Listed churn drivers (Mar 2026; unsourced percentages) are ROI not communicated (67%), slow responsiveness (58%), scope creep (52%) and no ongoing strategic guidance (47%). — [Ciela, AI agency client retention](https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-agency-client-retention)
- Clients cancel when they can't explain internally what they pay for, not because one month underperformed. Price is rarely the main driver for well-managed accounts. — [Cloud Campaign](https://www.cloudcampaign.com/answers/reduce-client-churn-social-retainers); [Agiled client retention stats 2026](https://agiled.app/statistics/client-retention-statistics)
- Voice-agent retainers are often cancelled around month 3 because the client "didn't see the value." Deeper workflow integration raises switching costs. A warning sign is no dashboard login in 30+ days (the source labels its ranking illustrative). — [Trillet](https://trillet.ai/blogs/voice-agent-client-churn-reduction)
- Early SMB churn warning signs (from an agency-platform vendor). — [Duda](https://blog.duda.co/warning-signs-your-smb-client-might-churn-and-how-to-keep-them); [AgencyAnalytics](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/reengaging-churned-customers)

### Inferences
- A $600/mo maintenance retainer with no reporting component reads to the client as "insurance." The sources suggest packaging it with a monthly outcome report (tasks automated, hours saved, leads or bookings captured) and a first-30/60/90-day value milestone, since the first 90 days carry most of the churn risk.
- $5K builds sit between the "single agent" ($1.5-5K) and "multi-workflow" ($5-12K) bands. Productized tiers could follow the market pattern: Starter (1 agent, 1 workflow), Growth (2-3 workflows plus integrations), and outcome add-ons.
- Many of these pricing blogs are themselves content marketing by AI agencies or tools. That suggests SEO and YouTube "how much does X cost" content works as an acquisition channel in this niche.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, methodologically sound 2025-2026 survey of AI-agency pricing. All figures are vendor or agency blogs.
- I did not find data-backed sources comparing acquisition channels for AI agencies (audits vs. demos vs. case studies vs. LinkedIn vs. YouTube). The research budget ran out before this topic was covered with citations, so treat channel claims as unverified.
- I found no measured monthly churn rates for AI maintenance retainers.

## 2. Women-entrepreneur SMB SaaS (Business Rehab.OS): market size, where they gather, pre-sell/founding-member tactics

### Takeaway
Women own roughly 39-41% of all US businesses, mostly non-employer firms (14.5-15.7M firms per Wells Fargo). They own about 1.4M employer firms (22.9%, Census ABS, ref. year 2023). Women-owned firms produce a disproportionately small share of revenue, which is the "revenue gap" positioning a business-management SaaS can speak to. Large free Facebook groups such as Women Helping Women Entrepreneurs (870K-980K members) are the biggest gathering places. Founding-member pre-sales capped at roughly 50-100 seats are a common validation path for small SaaS.

### Cited Findings
- **Wells Fargo 2025 Impact of Women-Owned Businesses report** (PDF dated Jan 2025, 2024 data):
  - 14.5M women-owned businesses, or 39.2% of US firms.
  - $3.3T in revenue, or 6.2% of total firm revenue.
  - Matching men-owned average revenue would add $10.2T a year.
  - Source: [Wells Fargo PDF](https://smallbusinessresources.wf.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/wells-fargo-2025-impact-of-women-owned-businesses.pdf).
- **Later Wells Fargo figures** (newer edition, as reported):
  - About 15.7M businesses, or 40.6% of all businesses.
  - They employ 12.6M people (9.1% of US employment).
  - $2.8T in revenue, or 4.6% of US business revenue.
  - A small subset of women-owned employer firms drives 82% of women-owned revenue.
  - Sources: [MBE Magazine](https://mbemag.com/articles/women-owned-businesses-are-growing-fast-but-still-face-a-10-trillion-revenue-gap/); [Wells Fargo resource page](https://wellsfargo.com/biz/wells-fargo-works/women-owned-business-resources); [Kathryn Finney newsletter](https://kathrynfinney.com/newsletters/the-receipts/women-own-40-percent-of-american-businesses).
  - The two editions conflict on revenue ($3.3T vs $2.8T), and the newer one's publication date (possibly Jan 2026) is unconfirmed. Verify before quoting.
- **Census Annual Business Survey (ref. year 2022):**
  - About 1.3M of 5.9M employer firms (22.3%) were women-owned.
  - About $2.1T in receipts, 11.4M employees and $508.5B in payroll.
  - Sources: [WIA Report (Jan 2025)](https://wiareport.com/2025/01/women-own-one-fifth-of-employer-businesses-in-the-united-states/); [Census 2024 employer businesses release](https://census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/employer-businesses.html).
- **Census ABS (ref. year 2023, released Nov 20, 2025):** about 1.4M of 5.9M employer firms (22.9%) are women-owned, per a search excerpt of the release. — [Census press release, Nov 20, 2025](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/business-owner-characteristics.html); [Census QuickFacts note](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/note/SBO020223)
- **Where women entrepreneurs gather online:**
  - Women Helping Women Entrepreneurs (Facebook) is described as the largest Facebook group for women entrepreneurs. Sources give 870K+ and 980K+ members. — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/online-communities-for-female-entrepreneurs/); [The Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/women-helping-women-entrepreneurs/)
  - The Female Entrepreneur Association (founded 2011) is called one of the largest global communities. — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/online-communities-for-female-entrepreneurs/)
  - The Hive Index lists about 20 female-founder communities across Facebook, Skool, Slack, Discord and Reddit. — [The Hive Index, Female Founders](https://thehiveindex.com/topics/female-founders/)
  - Gusto publishes a list of recommended Facebook groups for female entrepreneurs. — [Gusto](https://gusto.com/blog/growth/best-facebook-groups)
- **Pre-sell and founding-member tactics:**
  - Cap the offer at around 50-100 users and frame it as "founding member" rather than "lifetime." — [DEV Community thread](https://dev.to/vinzify/what-is-your-experience-in-launching-a-founder-edition-lifetime-program-51be)
  - Don't sell "lifetime" before you know your costs. — [3L3C, "10 Founding Members"](https://www.3l3c.ai/blog/us-startup-marketing-without-vc/founding-members-bootstrapped-saas)
  - A suggested sequence is waitlist, then a validation step, then a limited pre-sale. — [SaaS Factory Kit Substack](https://saasfactorykit.substack.com/p/getting-your-first-paying-users)
  - Founders report pre-selling lifetime deals to validate ideas, and about $3K in pre-sales was "enough to keep building." — [Product Hunt discussion](https://www.producthunt.com/p/self-promotion/pre-sell-your-saas-before-writing-a-single-line-of-code?page=2); [r/SaaS thread (mirror)](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/SaaS/comments/1jpmloz/built_bootstrapped_exited_2m_revenue_990k_appsumo/ml1bvsm/?context=3)
  - Encharge is an example of a pre-order lifetime-deal page from a real SaaS. — [Encharge pre-order](https://encharge.io/preorder/pre-order-paypal.html)

### Inferences
- About 22.9% of employer firms are women-owned, but about 40% of all firms are. So most of the target market is solo or non-employer businesses. A $99/mo price is plausible for that segment. A $395/mo add-on and a $3K setup likely fit the smaller employer-firm segment (about 1.4M firms).
- "Close the revenue gap" (the $10.2T figure) is a ready-made, citable positioning hook.
- A capped founding-member offer at the MVP stage could be a discounted monthly rate locked for life rather than a lifetime deal. This avoids the cost risk the sources warn about.

### Gaps
- The American Express "State of Women-Owned Businesses" report: no 2024-2026 edition turned up. It may have been discontinued after 2019 (unconfirmed).
- I found no SBA-specific 2025-2026 women-owned statistics in the results.
- Member counts for Hey Mama, IFundWomen, Ladies Get Paid and LinkedIn groups were not confirmed.
- I found no data on SaaS adoption or willingness to pay among women-owned SMBs.

## 3. Options education and finfluencers (Options Traders Academy): SEC, FINRA, FTC, CFTC, publisher's exclusion, disclaimers, platform policies, Skool benchmarks

### Takeaway
The biggest US enforcement risk for a trading-education seller is the FTC, not the SEC. The FTC has taken action against Raging Bull ($2.425M, 2022, plus a 2023 permanent injunction against trading guru Kyle Dennis) and Online Trading Academy ($362M judgment, mostly suspended, 2020). In both cases the problem was unsubstantiated "you'll make X" earnings claims, and Raging Bull's order also covered subscription and cancellation practices. A paid daily watchlist risks being treated as investment advice under the Advisers Act. The publisher's exclusion (Lowe v. SEC, 1985) protects only impersonal, disinterested, regularly circulated content, and it does not protect fraud. FINRA Rule 2210 binds broker-dealers and the influencers they pay, not independent educators, unless she takes paid broker or affiliate deals.

### Cited Findings
**FTC: trading-education enforcement and earnings claims**
- **RagingBull.com**
  - Sued in Dec 2020 under "Operation Income Illusion." The FTC alleged the gurus claimed subscribers could double or triple their accounts, that these results were not typical, and that the company never tracked subscriber results.
  - Settled in March 2022 for $2.425M. The order bars earnings claims without substantiation, requires affirmative consent for subscriptions, and requires an easier way to cancel.
  - About $2.4M was refunded to roughly 10,000 customers.
  - Sources: [FTC consumer alert (Mar 2022)](https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2022/03/investment-trading-operation-will-pay-millions-empty-promises); [Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/markets/raging-bull-customers-see-2-4m-refunds-ftc-dings-online-stock-trading-site); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/antitrust/investment-site-to-pay-ftc-2-4-million-for-earnings-deception).
- **Kyle Dennis (Raging Bull trading guru):** permanent injunction against future false earnings claims, Sept 2023. — [FTC press release, Sept 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/09/ragingbullcom-stock-trading-guru-kyle-dennis-faces-permanent-injunction-result-ftc-action) (page could not be fetched; summary from search excerpt)
- **Online Trading Academy**
  - Settled with the FTC in Sept 2020. The $362M judgment was partially suspended for inability to pay.
  - The company must offer debt forgiveness, and the founder and other individuals pay $5-$9.1M and turn over assets.
  - The FTC alleged the company falsely claimed purchasers were likely to earn significant income.
  - The order bans contracts that limit customers from talking to law enforcement or posting honest reviews.
  - Refunds of more than $5.4M were later sent.
  - Sources: [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/online-trading-academy-ftc-deceived-customers); [WTOP (Sept 2020)](https://wtop.com/government/2020/09/thousands-who-paid-for-get-rich-quick-training-program-can-have-debts-wiped-out-ftc-says); [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/ftc-sends-5-4-million-checks-victims-online-trading-academy/); [Law360](https://law360.com/amp/articles/1309860).
- **Notices of Penalty Offenses (Oct 2021):** sent to more than 1,100 companies, including investment and business coaching. They warned that false earnings claims, and misleading testimonials about the rewards of money-making opportunities, can bring civil penalties of up to $43,792 per violation (the 2021 figure, inflation-adjusted annually). — [FTC cover letter (MMO notice)](https://FTC.GOV/system/files/attachments/penalty-offenses-concerning-money-making-opportunities/cover-letter-mmo.pdf); [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/ftc-sends-notices-to-businesses-promoting-money-making-opportunities/)
- **January 2025 rulemaking proposals (3-2 vote):**
  - A new Earnings Claims Rule limited to MLMs.
  - An expansion of the Business Opportunity Rule to cover "money-making opportunities" such as business coaching and investment opportunities. It would require written substantiation for earnings claims and bar material misrepresentations.
  - I could not confirm whether either has been finalized.
  - Sources: [Hunton](https://hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/ftc-issues-three-notices-for-changes-to-the-business-opportunity-rule-and-a-new-rule-on-multi-level-marketing-mlm-practices); [JD Supra (2025)](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/targeting-mlms-and-other-money-making-4320927); [Mercatus](https://www.mercatus.org/research/policy-briefs/evaluating-ftcs-approach-deceptive-earnings-claims).

**SEC: Advisers Act and the publisher's exclusion**
- **Lowe v. SEC, 472 U.S. 181 (1985):**
  - The petitioners' newsletters fell within the "bona fide publication" exclusion, so the publisher was not an "investment adviser."
  - "Bona fide" refers to the content (disinterested commentary rather than promotional material), not the publisher's history.
  - Practitioners distill three criteria: genuineness, impersonal or disinterested advice, and general and regular circulation.
  - Sources: [Cornell LII opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181); [govinfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-472/pdf/USREPORTS-472-181.pdf); [MTSU First Amendment Encyclopedia](https://firstamendment.mtsu.edu/article/lowe-v-securities-and-exchange-commission/); [Interactive Brokers webinar, "Spotlight: publisher exclusion"](https://brokerage.ibkr.com/webinars/spotlight-publisher-exclusion.pdf).
- **SEC v. Wall Street Publishing Institute (D.D.C. 1986):** read Lowe as turning on disinterested content offered to the public on a regular schedule. Being a bona fide publication does not license fraud. — [Justia, 664 F. Supp. 554](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/664/554/1504347/)
- **In re Weiss Research:** the SEC sanctioned a newsletter publisher as an unregistered investment adviser. The exclusion applies only when communications between publisher and subscribers meet the criteria; the case involved an auto-trading service. — [Cahill memo](https://www.cahill.com/publications/firm-memoranda/0000001/_res/id=Attachments/index=0/CGR%20Firm%20Memo%20-%20In%20the%20Matter%20of%20Weiss%20Research%20Financial%20Newsletter%20Publisher%20Sanctioned%20as%20an%20Unregistered%20Investment%20Adviser.pdf)
- **SEC finfluencer enforcement and advisory work:**
  - Feb 16, 2024: settled action against a registered adviser for failing to disclose a social media influencer's role in an ETF launch ($1.75M fine plus censure).
  - The SEC Investor Advisory Committee has drafted recommendations for a finfluencer disclosure rule.
  - Sources: [Carlton Fields (2024)](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/expect-focus/2024/finra-and-sec-float-concerns-over-social-media-finfluencers); [Crowdfund Insider (IAC draft)](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=234005).
- **State regulators:** NASAA issued a 2022 advisory cautioning investors about finfluencers, and state regulators have issued similar ones. — [NASAA](https://www.nasaa.org/65026/nasaa-cautions-investors-on-the-rise-of-the-finfluencer/?qoid=newsroom); [North Dakota Securities Dept.](https://www.securities.nd.gov/sites/www/files/documents/investor-alerts/Investor%20Advisory%20-%20Finfluencers.pdf)

**FINRA: Rule 2210 and the finfluencer sweep**
- FINRA's sweep of firms' social media influencer and customer-acquisition practices began Sept 2021, with an update in Feb 2023.
  - Practices observed include written supervisory procedures for influencer programs, vetting influencers' past posts, and keeping records of influencer communications.
  - Posts count as firm retail communications when the firm paid for them or helped prepare them.
  - Sources: [FINRA sweep update (Feb 2023)](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/targeted-examination-letters/sweep-update-feb2023); [FINRA sweep letter](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/targeted-examination-letters/social-media-influencers-customer-acquisition-related-information-protection); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://nortonrosefulbright.com/en-ca/knowledge/publications/d3bed35e/finra-updates-its-sweep-regarding-social-media-influencers-referral-programs-and-privacy).
- Enforcement:
  - M1 Finance was fined $850,000 (2024) over its influencer program.
  - Other 2024 actions found influencer posts not fair or balanced, or exaggerated or promissory (Rules 2210 and 2010).
  - Sources: [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/?p=250948); [Troutman (May 22, 2024)](https://www.troutman.com/insights/influencer-considerations-as-finra-initiates-crackdown.html).
- FINRA has a podcast episode on its finfluencer targeted review. — [FINRA Unscripted](https://www.finra.org/media-center/finra-unscripted/finfluencer-social-media-targeted-review)
- FINRA reported a spike in complaints about fraudulent investment groups promoted on social media, including encrypted group chats (Dec 2025, per secondary source). — [securitieslawyer101 (2025)](https://www.securitieslawyer101.com/2025/regulation-of-financial-influencers/)

**Platform policies**
- **Meta:** financial products and services ads are restricted, not banned, and advertisers may need to show regulatory authorization. Since Jan 14, 2025, US-based or US-targeted financial products and services campaigns must use the Special Ad Category, which limits targeting. — [Meta Transparency Center, Financial services ad standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/financial-services); [Meta Marketing API, Special Ad Category](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category)
- **Meta income claims:** Meta's ad standards restrict income-claim and "get rich quick" ad creative (a secondary summary was found but the policy text was not fetched). — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/meta-ads-income-claims-policy)
- **TikTok:** since 2021, branded content (creator-brand paid partnerships) may not promote financial services, including management of financial assets, crypto, loans, BNPL and get-rich-quick schemes. Paid ads are governed separately by TikTok's ad policies. I could not confirm whether this is unchanged in 2026. — [Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/tiktok-prohibits-financial-services-and-products-branded-content-693178); [FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/retail-forex/tiktok-bans-forex-cfds-and-crypto-trading-from-branded-content/)
- **YouTube:** the Spam, deceptive practices and scams policy prohibits promoting "get rich quick" investment schemes and pyramid schemes. Violations can suspend monetization or terminate the channel. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973)
- **Threads:** I found no Threads-specific financial-content policy (see Gaps).

**Skool monetization benchmarks** (third-party estimates; Skool publishes none)
- The median paid Skool community charges $33/mo; the most common band is $26-50. Median estimated MRR is about $1,217/mo (price times members, so a ceiling) (June 2026). — [Profitable.app Skool stats](https://profitable.app/skool/stats)
- In the "Money" category (July 2026, 1,000 trending communities), 85.7% charge, at a $29 median. The median trending group (205 members at $27) grosses about $5,535/mo. — [RevenueGeeks](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/statistics)
- A blog citing Latka gives an average paid price of $376.77/mo, which is skewed by outliers. — [AI Funnel Insider price index](https://aifunnelinsider.com/skool-community-price-index/); [Communipass benchmarks 2026](https://communipass.com/?p=20194)
- Pro-plan fee example: 2.9% transaction fee plus $99/mo. — [Profitable.app calculator](https://profitable.app/tools/skool-revenue-calculator)

### Inferences
- **Earnings claims and testimonials.** Win-rate screenshots, "I made $X" posts and member P&L testimonials used to sell the $59, $99 or $30/day products are the pattern the FTC attacked in Raging Bull and Online Trading Academy. Claims need substantiation of typical results, and an "results not typical" line alone does not cure a deceptive testimonial.
- **Subscriptions and cancellation.** The Raging Bull order shows the FTC also targets subscription practices: affirmative consent and easy cancellation. That applies directly to the $59/mo, $99/mo and $30/day recurring products. Federal "click-to-cancel" rule status was not checked in this research; see Gaps.
- **Keeping the watchlist on the publisher side of the line:**
  - Keep it impersonal: the same list for all subscribers, no individualized advice and no DMs answering "should I buy X."
  - Publish on a regular schedule.
  - Disclose any positions she holds, and avoid trading ahead of the list.
  - Do not offer auto-trading or copy-trading.
  - Personalized 1:1 coaching on specific trades moves toward Advisers Act territory. State-level adviser rules may also apply.
- **Disclaimers** commonly used in practice: education only, not investment advice, not a registered adviser or broker-dealer, options involve substantial risk of loss and are not suitable for all investors, past performance is not indicative of future results, and disclosure of the author's positions. These are industry practice, not a sourced regulatory requirement (see Gaps).
- **Platforms.** Organic YouTube and Threads content that avoids "get rich quick" framing is the safest acquisition channel. Paid Meta ads for the trading products will likely be routed into the Special Ad Category with limited targeting. TikTok brand-paid financial partnerships are off-limits.
- **Pricing benchmarks.** A $99/mo Skool community is about 3x the platform's median price but well below the skewed mean. That is reasonable if it is paired with the $59 app and positioned as premium.

### Gaps
- **CFTC:** I found no sourced CFTC material for this scope. Listed equity and index options are SEC/FINRA-regulated. The CFTC would matter only if the content covers futures or options on futures (possible commodity trading advisor (CTA) registration questions). This was not researched with citations.
- I did not retrieve the exact statutory text of Advisers Act §202(a)(11)(D). Lowe is cited instead.
- I found no primary SEC investor alert on finfluencers (dated 2024-2026). The secondary claim of a Feb 2026 SEC warning is unverified.
- I found no primary source specifying required disclaimers for non-registered trading educators. Disclaimer practice is listed as an inference only.
- Status of the FTC's Jan 2025 Business Opportunity Rule expansion: unknown.
- Status of the FTC's 2024 "Click-to-Cancel" (Negative Option) rule: not researched here. A court reportedly vacated it in 2025 (unverified in this session).
- Threads-specific financial content policy: not found. Threads presumably falls under Meta Community Guidelines (unverified).
- Any FTC trading-education actions dated 2024-2026 beyond the refunds were not found.

## 4. FTC sponsored content disclosure and micro-influencer sponsorship rates (rate cards)

### Takeaway
The FTC's revised Endorsement Guides (final June 30, 2023; 16 C.F.R. Part 255) require clear and conspicuous disclosure of any material connection, including free products. A disclosure is not "unavoidable" if it is hidden behind a "more" link. The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 C.F.R. Part 465, effective Oct 21, 2024) bans fake or manipulated reviews and testimonials. Micro-influencer (10K-100K) rates vary more than 10x across sources: roughly $150-$2,000 per Instagram post, TikTok usually 10-20% lower, and YouTube long-form about $2K-$10K or CPM-based.

### Cited Findings
- The FTC finalized revisions to the Endorsement Guides on June 30, 2023, the first full update since 2009. — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/en/news-insights/ftc-finalizes-updated-guidance-for-influencer-and-consumer-review-programs.html); [Morgan Lewis (Jul 2023)](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2023/07/ftc-updates-endorsement-guides-proposes-endorsement-related-rule)
- **Key provisions:**
  - Material connections between endorser and advertiser must be disclosed.
  - An influencer who receives an unsolicited free product and is asked, but not required, to post still makes an endorsement.
  - The guides now define "clear and conspicuous." A disclosure behind a "more" link is not "unavoidable."
  - Virtual influencers are covered.

### Inferences
- **Rate card.** The Options Traders Academy creator should anchor YouTube integrations on CPM of actual average views, and Threads or short-form posts on Instagram-equivalent tiers. Finance is a premium niche, so the upper ends of ranges are defensible.
- **Disclosure.** Every sponsored integration needs an in-video, upfront disclosure ("#ad" or "Paid partnership"), not one buried in the description behind "more." Affiliate links to brokers count as material connections.
- **Stacked compliance on broker sponsorships.** A paid broker-dealer sponsorship also pulls the content into FINRA Rule 2210, because the firm paid for the post. The sponsor will likely require pre-approval and fair-and-balanced risk language. TikTok prohibits financial-services branded content outright.
